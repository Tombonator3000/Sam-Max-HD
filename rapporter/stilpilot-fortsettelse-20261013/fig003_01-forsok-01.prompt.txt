Input: the attached image is the original low-resolution sheet enlarged exactly 4x with hard pixel edges. The frames stand apart on a plain flat cyan background (#00C8C8). Around the sheet is a plain gray (#808080) border. The background and the border are only padding.

What the sheet shows: Max, the white rabbit-like creature (long ears, wide toothy grin, no clothes), standing, walking and talking. The description is only a help; the input image decides. On this sheet, row by row from left to right: standing, facing right, toward the viewer, away from the viewer (3 frames); start pose (a separate part the game draws on top of the body, such as the head), facing right, toward the viewer, away from the viewer (3 frames); start pose (a separate part the game draws on top of the body, such as the head), facing right, toward the viewer (2 frames); walking, facing right (8 frames); talking (a separate part the game draws on top of the body, such as the head), facing right (12 frames). The game mirrors the frames facing right when the character faces left, so there are no frames facing left; do not add any.

Size rule (most important): draw every frame at exactly the size it has in the input, with the same height, the same width and the same proportions of head, body, arms, legs and props. One input pixel is 4 output pixels for every frame on this sheet and on every other sheet of this character, so the character never grows, shrinks or changes shape between frames. On this sheet the frames are from 8 to 216 pixels tall and from 56 to 196 pixels wide in the input (the box around each frame's visible pixels); jobb.json lists every frame's box under ruter (synlig, in original pixels: multiply by 4).

Do:
- Trace every frame at 4x in high resolution, so it looks like the characters of Day of the Tentacle Remastered next to their originals: keep the pose, silhouette and proportions, and turn every cluster of pixels into the shape it shows (glasses, a tie, a shoe). Clean dark anti-aliased outlines with a little variation in weight, flat colors with one shade and a few highlights, the same color for every area as in the input. No dithering, no texture, no gradients, no pixel steps.
- Keep the original design: the same character, the same colors, light direction, shadows and contrast as in the input.
- Make all frames look identical apart from the pose: the same face, eyes, hat, clothes, colors and light in every frame. Any difference between frames flickers in the game.
- Keep the pose and silhouette of every frame, and keep each frame in its place on the sheet, in the same rows and the same order.
- Glowing effects (light, lightning, sparks, fire) are painted as soft glow that fades into transparency.

Do not:
- Do not add, remove, merge, split or redesign frames.
- Do not add anything that is not in the input: no new props, accessories, patterns, logos, text, shadows, floor, numbers, labels, frames or grid lines.
- Do not change colors, clothes or proportions, and do not make heads, hands or eyes bigger than in the input.
- Some frames show only part of the character (a body without a head, a head alone, an arm), because the game draws the parts separately. Paint them as they are and do not complete them.
- Do not let frames touch or overlap each other.
- No photorealism, no modern 3D look, no film grain, no vignette, no watermark, no signature.
